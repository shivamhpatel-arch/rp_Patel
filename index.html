Structural Stability of Dwarf Gray Sugar Pea Plants

Measuring the Bend Angle, Breaking Force, and Sugar Levels after Decapitation at Different Times

Background

Dwarf Gray Sugar Pea Plants are small plants that are easy to grow and do not require much space, but when grown in large quantities, they are prone to lodging, which is when standing plants flatten due to rain or wind, making them more likely to break. The use of decapitation to prevent this has not been tested before. Decapitation, the process of cutting the apical bud off the plant's main stem, reduces plant height promotes lateral growth, and redistributed hormones and materials. Optimizing this could lead to increased growth rates and structual stability.

Rationale

  • Few studies examine the effects of the timing of pruning in plants, and those that do exist do not focus on structural stability.
  • Incorrectly pruning plants or pruning at the wrong time could lead to decreased structural stability.
  • Studying sucrose levels provided insight on plant growth patterns.
  • Understanding how the timing of decapitation affects sugar levels and structural stability can help researchers manipulate pruning timing to produce pea plants with specific growth patterns.
  • This could help gardeners as it has been shown that greater stability in pea plants leads to more reliable yields.

Objective

This study aimed to answer the following question: How does decapitating dwarf grey sugar pea plants at different times affect the structural stability of the plant?

Goals:

  • Determine the optimal decapitation timing
  • Find how sucrose allocation affects structural stability
  • Find whether breaking force or bend angle are better for lodging prevention

Hypotheses & Variables

Hypothesis: Decapitating at different times will affect structural stability.
Prediction: Pruning after four weeks would lead to the greatest stability.

Independent Variable: Timing of decapitation
Dependent Variables: Structural stability and Sucrose levels
